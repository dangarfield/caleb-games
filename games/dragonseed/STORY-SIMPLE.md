# Dragonseed — the simple story

Same game, same plants, same shop. New story, six people, plain words.

Nothing in this file is built yet. It is here to be argued with.

---

## The whole thing in four sentences

You keep the plant shop in a valley where everybody has a dragon.
One night you find a warm, shiny egg in your cellar.
A very big dragon is looking for it, and the village wants her driven away.
You have sixteen days to decide what to do with the egg.

That is the story. A child can repeat it after hearing it once, which is the
test the old one failed.

---

## What goes

Out: the dead grandmother, her great-aunt, the book that isn't yours, the
letters, the ledger, the museum, the Ember Sisters, the Bell, the Hatch, the
parish vote, the muster, the rites, the collaring notice, the masked woman, and
twenty-five one-line walk-ons.

**The shop is simply yours.** You are the plant shop. Nobody died. There is no
inheritance to explain and no relative to remember.

**The book is simply your book.** Somebody before you wrote the pages; you do
not need to know who. When a page turns up in a drawer, it is a page you did not
have yet — no history lesson attached.

---

## Six people

Each one has one job, one thing they want, and one way of talking. They come
back again and again, so by day four Caleb knows them.

| | who they are | what they want | how they talk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ivo** | Dragon farmer. Forty-one dragons in his yard, and he knows every one by its noise. | His dragons well. Later: the big dragon left alone. | Warm, blunt, funny. Knows more about dragons than anyone. |
| **Doctor Wren** | The village doctor. | Medicine for people, quickly. | Short. Always in a hurry. Says thank you properly. |
| **Nan** | Old, walks everywhere, has been up every hill in the valley. | You to get out of the shop and go and look. | Kind. Tells you where things grow. Gives you the map and the notes. |
| **Mrs Coyle** | A dragon bit her hand when she was small. | The big dragon gone. She is frightened, not nasty. | Careful, firm. Says "I want to be plain." |
| **Tom** | A boy your age. Runs messages, works at the forge. | To know what you are hiding. Then to help. | Fast, nosy, loyal. |
| **The Warden** | Comes from the town to sort out the dragon problem. | The truth, written down. | Polite. Asks the question you don't want. |

Two more characters who never stand at your counter:

- **The big dragon.** Made of something like glass. You see her four times and
  she never once attacks anybody.
- **The baby.** In the egg for eleven days. Then it is out, and it is the size
  of a cat, and it knows you.

---

## Sixteen days, one line each

Every day says its own thing at the top of the screen, and the story beat is
said **out loud at the counter** — not hidden in a note.

| day | what happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | You open the shop. Four people want plants. That night, you find the egg. |
| 2 | Nan gives you the map. Somebody saw something enormous over the moor. |
| 3 | Nan brings you a drawer of old notes. The egg was cold this morning and warm by noon. |
| 4 | The moor burns. Nobody is hurt. Something is looking for something. |
| 5 | Mrs Coyle wants a plant that keeps dragons out of a field. She is scared. |
| 6 | **Ivo says it out loud: only a glass dragon lays an egg like that, and only one, ever.** So the big dragon is its mother. |
| 7 | Tom gets two steps down your cellar stairs. **You choose: tell him, or send him away.** |
| 8 | The lake is empty. She is looking underneath everything. |
| 9 | The Warden arrives and starts asking people questions. |
| 10 | The egg rocks. Nan: "Eggs hatch when they are ready and not before." |
| 11 | The Warden asks you straight out. **You choose: tell him, or lie.** |
| 12 | At two in the morning it hatches. It is out, it is warm, and it follows you. |
| 13 | The village votes to drive her off. Mrs Coyle asks you for something to help them. Ivo asks you to help him stop them. |
| 14 | The baby cries all night and she comes closer. You need something to settle it. |
| 15 | **You choose: climb up and see her, or stay in.** If you go, you find she is not angry. She is looking. |
| 16 | Everyone is in the square. **Carry the baby up the hill and give it back — or go out the back and keep it.** |

Four choices in sixteen days, each one a plain either/or, each one remembered at
the end in one sentence. Not nineteen hidden flags.

## How it sounds now

The same customers, rewritten. Old on the left, new on the right.

**Day 1, Maren Ash**

> was: *"My little one hasn't slept in four nights. Nor has her dragon, and it sleeps on her bed, so you can imagine."*
>
> now: **"My little girl can't sleep. Four nights now. Have you got something for sleep?"**

**Day 2, Ivo**

> was: *"Two of my lads went at each other over a hen — my sons, not my dragons. One's fine and one is not."*
>
> now: **"My two boys had a fight over a hen. One of them has a cut on his arm. Something to close it up?"**

**Day 5, Mrs Coyle**

> was: *"I want to be plain. Something that would turn a large animal away from a field without touching it."*
>
> now: **"I want to be plain. Something that keeps a big animal out of a field. I don't want it hurt. I want it gone."**

**Day 6, Ivo — the reveal**

> now: **"Listen. A dragon like mine lays six eggs a year. A glass dragon lays one egg. One, in her whole life. If somebody has a glass egg, then somewhere there is a mother looking for it, and she will not stop."**

**Day 9, the Warden**

> now: **"Good morning. Something very large is breaking this valley up and I have been sent to find out why. I shall ask everybody the same question. Have you seen anything you can't explain?"**

**Evening 12, it hatches**

> now: **"Two in the morning. No fuss at all. The shell comes apart like a nut and there it is, the size of a cat, wet and cross and warm. It looks at you for a long time. Then it climbs into your coat and goes to sleep."**

Rules I am writing to: short sentences; one idea each; no *parish*, *muster*,
*ordinance*, *rite*, *apothecary*, *poultice*, *fortnight*; say **village**,
**the men with nets**, **the new rule**, **shop**, **two weeks**. Nobody speaks
more than three short sentences at the counter.

---

## The end

Two endings, and it says which one you got in words a child would use.

**You gave her back her egg.** You carry the basket up the hill with the whole
village watching. She comes down to meet you. She takes the baby the way a cat
carries a kitten, and she is gone before the church clock finishes striking.
The shop opens at nine. Everybody asks you about it for the rest of your life.

**You kept it.** Out the back, under your coat, three fields away before anybody
notices the shop is empty. It grows up knowing you and only you. Somewhere
behind you a glass dragon is still looking, and she will never stop, because
that is the thing about mothers.

Then up to three extra lines, only for things Caleb will remember doing:

- *You told Tom, and he never told anybody. He still brings the post.*
- *You lied to the Warden. He wrote it down as true and thanked you.*
- *You went up the crags on your own on the Friday night, and stood close enough to hear her breathe.*

---

## What this costs

- 124 customer visits stay. Their **plants** stay. Only the **words** change,
  and the six regulars take over the visits the twenty-five walk-ons had.
- The four opening panels become three, and lose the family history.
- The sixteen evenings stay but get shorter: one small scene, five or six lines.
- The fourteen notes in the drawer become **eight**, and each one is either a
  place to go or a page for the book. No backstory, no letters.
- Nineteen flags become **four choices**.
- Everything in DEPENDENCIES.html can be typed over afterwards, so you get the
  final pass on every line.
