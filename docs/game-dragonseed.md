# Dragonseed

(Folder and doc are named `dragonseed`; the user-facing title is **Dragonseed**,
subtitle "Sixteen days at the plant shop".)

A **deduction / identification** game in the mould of *Strange Horticulture* — an
original take on the genre, not a copy. You keep the plant shop in a valley where
everybody has a dragon. People come in and describe a plant, or describe a
problem, **without naming what they want**; you study the pot on your desk under a
lens, cross-reference an illustrated **book**, put a name to it, and hand it over.
The verb is **deduce, not fetch** — the correct pot is never highlighted.

On the first night you find a warm egg in the cellar, far too big for any dragon
in the valley to have laid. Something very large is looking for it, and the
village wants her driven off. You have sixteen days to decide what to do with the
egg. Three choices along the way and what you do on the last night decide which
of three endings you get.

**Originality.** Every plant name, pseudo-Latin binomial, description, character,
place and the region itself (an invented moorland valley, "Morrowfen") is
original. Only the genre's *mechanics and UI structure* are reused — mechanics
are not copyrightable. Research that informed the systems is in
`research/dragonseed/`.

**Landscape only.** The shop is shelves, desk and counter side by side; a portrait
phone gets a "turn it sideways" card instead of a squeezed layout.

## Features

- **One draggable desk, three columns.** Shelves down the left (drag a pot onto the
  desk), the desk in the middle (the lens, and the book and map overlays open here),
  the counter down the right (a customer, and a drop zone — drag the pot onto them
  to hand it over). No tabs on a landscape screen; below 860px it collapses to one
  region at a time with a tab bar.
- **82 specimens, 17 locations, 16 days, 124 visits, six recurring characters.**
  Nan, Ivo, Tom, Mrs Coyle, Doctor Wren and the Warden come back all fortnight, so
  by day four you know them.
- **The lens.** A specimen on the desk starts unnamed. You reveal one attribute at a
  time — colour, form, petals, leaf, stem, scent, berry, mark — and each observation
  you tick becomes a filter on the book.
- **The book.** Every plant has a page from the start; the puzzle was never "guess
  what it is called", it is "work out which of these is the one on my desk". You can
  filter by up to three observations, search the words a customer used, and claim a
  page to name the specimen. Guessing wrong crosses that page out for the rest of the
  day and costs a pip of confusion.
- **Confusion, and the spell of calm.** Three pips — from wrong names, paid hints and
  wasted trips — and you cast the spell of calm: six carved runes handed to you out of
  order, dragged back into order fewest marks to most. Then the pips clear and the day
  carries on. It is friction, not a story: nothing else in the game reads it.
- **The map.** Being told about a place gives you a *lead* — a sentence describing
  where it is. You read the drawn map of Morrowfen, work out the lettered-and-numbered
  square, and click it. Right, and the place is pinned for good. Twelve places are shut
  and want a plant before they will let you in; behind each is the only cutting of
  something somebody asks for later.
- **The drawer.** Fourteen notes arrive over the fortnight and lie loose in the drawer
  under the counter, each on its own painted sheet. Drag them about, click one to open
  it, click again to put it down. Reading one gives you book pages and directions.
- **The evenings.** Sixteen full-screen story panels between the days. Seven of them
  the egg (later the hatchling) wants something and you pick a named cutting to give
  it — the same verb as serving a customer, pointed at the thing under your floor.
  Getting it wrong costs nothing but a long night.
- **Forks.** Some visitors accept two different plants with different consequences.
  The panel says what each would *do*, never which plant it is.
- **The art.** 254 webp pictures — a colour plate and a line-drawn book plate for
  every plant, eight faces, 24 views of the locations in the state the story has left
  them in, 22 story panels, the furniture, and a painted sheet for each note. The game
  runs with all of it, some of it, or none: everything falls back to a generated SVG
  plate. `js/art.js` is generated by scanning `art/`.
- **Easy plant mode** — off by default, behind the `?` in the top bar. On, the book
  shows the colour plates instead of the line drawings.
- **Three walkthroughs** — the book on day 1, the map on day 2, the drawer on day 3.
  A card parked in a bottom corner that moves to the other corner when you go near it,
  flashing the control each step is about.
- **Saves.** IndexedDB via `ArcadeStore`, key `calebArcadeData:dragonseed`, plus one
  snapshot per customer behind the day in the top bar for debugging.

## File Structure

Multi-file under `games/dragonseed/`. The game is `index.html`, `css/`, `js/` and
`art/` — about 22MB, and it runs opened straight off the filesystem.

- `index.html` — shell: start screen, the turn-it-sideways card, topbar, the
  three-column board, mobile tabs. Loads scripts in order.
- `js/data.js`, `js/art.js` — **GENERATED. Do not hand-edit.**
- `js/engine.js` — state, save/load, the drag controller, the region registry.
- `js/clues.js` — deduction helpers. No DOM, no state.
- `js/shelf.js`, `js/desk.js`, `js/visitor.js` — the three columns.
- `js/book.js`, `js/map.js`, `js/mapart.js`, `js/look.js` — the book, the chart, and
  the look-closer lightbox.
- `js/story.js`, `js/rite.js`, `js/modal.js`, `js/coach.js`, `js/topbar.js`,
  `js/plate.js`, `js/store.js`, `js/debug.js`, `js/game.js`.
- `build/` — the Python content pipeline. `validate.py` proves the fortnight is
  solvable, `emit.py` writes `js/data.js`, `deps.py` writes `DEPENDENCIES.html`,
  `artmanifest.py` writes `js/art.js`, `artprompts.py` writes the image prompts.
- `tests/` — fifteen Playwright scripts and how to run them. Never part of the site.
- `research/` — art direction and source images. Gitignored, and it must stay that
  way: the asset zip alone is 170MB.
- `README.md` — the real architecture document. `STORY-SIMPLE.md` is the story
  standard. `DEPENDENCIES.html` is the generated editing surface.

## Key Design Decisions

- **Every word the player can read is editable in one place.** `DEPENDENCIES.html` is
  generated by `build/deps.py` — 691 editable lines, the unlock chains day by day.
  Edit there, export, and `build/apply_edits.py` folds it back into the Python tables.
- **Content is proved solvable before it ships.** `build/validate.py` checks that
  every request resolves, that every plant is reachable, that forks have exactly two
  answers, and that quoted lines exist on the pages they quote.
- **The art is optional.** Nothing hard-codes a filename. `Art.plant(id)` and friends
  return null when a picture is missing and the caller draws the generated plate
  instead, so the game is never broken by an image that is not there yet.
- **Where the art is served from is one variable.** `js/art.js` builds every URL from
  a base that defaults to `art/`; set `window.DRAGONSEED_ART_BASE` before it loads to
  move them. Nine `background-image` rules in `css/style.css` name `../art/` directly
  and are NOT covered by it.
- **Confusion never ends the day.** The original genre model resets the day at three
  pips. That is a punishment an eight-year-old feels as unfairness, so the day always
  carries on and the cost is a small tedious puzzle instead.
- **A page tells you what it is; a pot is the plant.** A cutting handed over the
  counter comes with its leaf, because whoever brings it knows what it is. A cutting
  you dig up yourself does not — which is why gathering fills the shelf with unnamed
  pots and the drawer of loose leaves is what turns them into stock.

## Memory

- **Built 2026-09-02** from the `/new-game` flow, as *Potions*, at reduced scope
  (19 flowers, 10 customers) on a four-tab layout.
- **Rebuilt 2026-09-03** to the real single-view desk, then to the full fortnight:
  82 specimens, 14 locations, 16 days, a validated content pipeline in Python, and
  generated SVG plate art.
- **Story rewritten (simple).** The first story had a dead grandmother, an inherited
  book, a museum, a cult, letters, a ledger and twenty-five one-line walk-ons. It was
  rewritten to six people and four sentences a child can repeat after hearing them
  once — see `STORY-SIMPLE.md`. The shop is simply yours; nobody died.
- **Renamed to Dragonseed** from *Potions*. Save key moved to
  `calebArcadeData:dragonseed`.
- **254 pictures added** and the SVG plates became the fallback rather than the
  artwork. Source PNGs converted to webp: 164MB → 22MB.
- **"Dread" became "confusion" and the rite became the spell of calm** — the meter is
  `state.confusion`, capped at `Engine.MUDDLE_CAP`, fed by `Engine.addMuddle()`.
  Note that "dread" survives as a plant EFFECT ("fills the drinker with a dread they
  cannot name") on two plants and one shut place. That is flavour and unrelated.
- **Fork replies were blank for six visitors.** `Clues.forkOutcome()` returns the fork
  option, whose words are in `reply`; `give()` read `out.text`, which does not exist.
  Days 2, 7, 10, 12, 14 and the ending on 16 all handed over an empty card. Fixed.
- **Pages used to arrive in silence.** 57 book pages arrive as pages alone across 16
  gifts on days 2–14. They now share the arrival card with any cuttings, drawn as the
  book's own line plates on paper.
- **The start screen does not teach the controls.** It says what the game is; the
  three walkthroughs and the `?` in the top bar teach the shop, in the shop.
